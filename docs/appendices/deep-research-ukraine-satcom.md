# Ukraine's Own Sky: The Nokia–ICEYE Sovereign Satellite Deal, the Starlink Shock That Preceded It, and What Comes Next

## Executive Summary

On **22 October 2026**, during Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo's visit to Kyiv, Ukraine's Ministry of Defence signed a **Letter of Intent with Finnish companies ICEYE and Nokia** to explore building a **sovereign low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite communications capability** — a system Ukraine itself would own and control, from the satellites to the ground segment to the user terminals ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-with-the-ministry-of-defence-of-ukraine-to-develop-sovereign-satcom-capability), [Nokia](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/iceye-and-nokia-partner-with-the-ministry-of-defence-of-ukraine-to-develop-sovereign-satcom-capability/)). The timing is no coincidence: the LoI landed in the middle of a ten-day crisis in which SpaceX's military-grade **Starshield service was throttled over Ukraine without warning**, crippling the drone operations that depend on it. This report lays out what the deal actually is (and is not), why the October Starshield incident made it urgent, what each partner genuinely brings, how it compares with Starlink, OneWeb and the EU's IRIS², and what has to happen for Ukraine to turn a letter of intent into its own constellation.

The short version: **this is real, it matters, and it is not yet a constellation**. A Letter of Intent commits nobody to procurement, no money or schedule for Ukraine was disclosed, and the first ICEYE–Nokia communications satellites are only expected in **2028** ([Telecoms Tech News](https://www.telecomstechnews.com/news/iceye-and-nokia-sign-ukraine-sovereign-satellite-letter-of-intent/)). But the underlying capability is unusually credible: ICEYE has already delivered a full sovereign radar-satellite system to Poland **in under 12 months**, has supported Ukraine since 2022 (including the crowdfunded "People's Satellite"), and Nokia brings the secure networking, tactical-radio and standards-layer technology — plus a defense organization chaired by a dedicated geopolitical officer — to build the ground and terminal layers ([PR Newswire/ICEYE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/iceye-delivers-mikrosar-system-to-polish-armed-forces-in-under-12-months-302773631.html), [Nokia](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers/)).

## 1. What Actually Happened: The Deal, in Facts

### 1.1 Two announcements, three weeks apart

The deal has two layers that are easy to conflate. On **1 October 2026**, ICEYE and Nokia announced a corporate partnership to jointly develop **secure, sovereign, high-performance broadband LEO satellite communications systems for governments**, covering missions from defense and border security to emergency services and disaster response, with the first communications satellites expected to launch in **2028** ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers)). The defining design principle is ownership: the jointly developed systems give nations control of **all three layers — the satellites, the ground segment, and the user terminals** — a direct alternative to renting capacity on a foreign-operated constellation whose vendor sets the continuity terms ([Telecoms Tech News](https://www.telecomstechnews.com/news/iceye-and-nokia-sign-ukraine-sovereign-satellite-letter-of-intent/)).

The second layer is Ukraine-specific: on **22 October 2026**, the two companies signed a **Letter of Intent with the Ministry of Defence of Ukraine** "to explore cooperation in space," announced during Prime Minister Orpo's Kyiv visit, one of seven documents signed that day within a package of agreements totalling **€300 million** — though no financial value was attributed to the ICEYE–Nokia document itself ([Nordic Defence Sector](https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/iceye-and-nokia-sign-loi-with-ukraine-on-sovereign-satellite-communications), [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/air/ukraine-explores-sovereign-satellite-network-with-iceye-and-nokia)). President Zelenskyy framed it in sovereignty terms: "Modern warfare has proven that reliable communications are a matter of national security. We must all rely on capabilities that we can control" ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-with-the-ministry-of-defence-of-ukraine-to-develop-sovereign-satcom-capability)).

### 1.2 What a Letter of Intent is — and is not

Precision matters here, because headlines calling this a "Starlink alternative for Ukraine" run ahead of the document. The LoI is **explicitly exploratory**: it does not bind either company to a procurement or deployment contract, and no pricing, delivery timetable, spacecraft count, ground-station plan or terminal volumes were disclosed ([Telecoms Tech News](https://www.telecomstechnews.com/news/iceye-and-nokia-sign-ukraine-sovereign-satellite-letter-of-intent/), [Nordic Defence Sector](https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/iceye-and-nokia-sign-loi-with-ukraine-on-sovereign-satellite-communications)). Nor has any Ukraine-specific launch schedule been announced — the 2028 date applies to the companies' broader joint development program, not to a Ukrainian constellation ([Nordic Defence Sector](https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/iceye-and-nokia-sign-loi-with-ukraine-on-sovereign-satellite-communications)).

What the LoI *does* do is give Ukraine's defense ministry **an option on a sovereign satcom pathway** with two European vendors who are already building the underlying system for the general government market. Turning that option into hardware requires program funding, spectrum coordination, and a trained operator cadre — all outside the letter's scope ([Telecoms Tech News](https://www.telecomstechnews.com/news/iceye-and-nokia-sign-ukraine-sovereign-satellite-letter-of-intent/)). Both companies describe the architecture as **complementing** military and commercial networks rather than replacing them — adding reach, resilience and capacity when terrestrial networks are disrupted, not substituting for Starlink overnight ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers)).

## 2. Why Now: The Ten-Day Starshield Crisis

### 2.1 What happened over Ukraine, 14–24 October 2026

In the early hours of **14 October 2026**, Ukrainian drone operators detected that Starshield — the militarized, jam-resistant tier of SpaceX's Starlink network — had stopped working at previously supported speeds: the threshold at which terminals lost connectivity collapsed from around **470 km/h to as low as 60 km/h**, before being partially raised to about **161 km/h** later that day ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813), [Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/spacex-starlink-speed-ukraine-drones/)). The cap may sound generous until you know what flies faster: Ukraine's interceptor drones hunting jet-powered Shaheds, and the mid-range "Middle Strike" drones hitting Russian logistics 50–300 km behind the front, cruise at or above 160–180 km/h. Ukraine's 7th Rapid Reaction Corps reported cancelling missions; units saw link drops of 10–20 seconds at just 110–120 km/h ([Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/spacex-starlink-speed-ukraine-drones/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/)).

The battlefield cost was immediate and measurable. The Institute for the Study of War recorded that data from Ukraine's Unmanned Systems Forces showed the restriction **significantly degraded the intermediate-range strike campaign** against Russian logistics between 14 and 19 October, and a Ukrainian drone-pilot analyst estimated the degradation cost **tens of millions of dollars** ([ISW](https://x.com/TheStudyofWar/status/2112730962148601997), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/)). Zelenskyy said the caps prevented Ukraine from using Starshield to shoot down Shahed attack drones, and warned that "the Russians are trying to take advantage" ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/)).


### 2.2 The accountability fog — and why that's the real lesson

The most strategically important feature of the crisis is that **nobody could say who ordered it**. Reuters cited Western officials pointing to the White House; the Wall Street Journal and CBS sources pointed to Elon Musk personally; Musk said limits are "decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS"; Secretary of State Rubio said the administration could not direct a private company; Trump said he was "seeking details on who ordered the slowdown" — and then, on 22 October, announced that Musk had "put it back," which Ukrainian testing that morning showed was not yet true ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/), [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/starlink-still-restricted-for-ukraine-donald-trump-elon-musk-lifted-speed-limits/)). Full service was only restored on **24 October**, after ten days ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813)).

Four US senators demanded explanations; Republican Rep. Mike Turner called for an inspector-general investigation, noting "lives are at risk and lives are lost over these things" ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/elon-musk-ukraine-spacex-starshield-trump-administration/)). The episode converted an abstract risk — dependence on a foreign billionaire's terms of service — into a demonstrated battlefield vulnerability with a price tag. It was during exactly this window, on the day Trump claimed restoration, that the Ukraine–ICEYE–Nokia LoI was signed in Kyiv. The sequence is the story: **Ukraine's leadership is buying insurance against the next unexplained throttle**, not reacting to this one alone.

## 3. The Dependence Problem, Quantified

### 3.1 How deep Starlink runs in Ukraine's war machine

Starlink is not a convenience in Ukraine; it is critical infrastructure. Terminals arrived within days of the February 2022 invasion — **more than 5,000 were operating by mid-March 2022** — and the network has since carried everything from continuity-of-government communications and hospital connectivity to battlefield command-and-control and drone data links ([CircleID](https://circleid.com/posts/starlink-in-ukraine-what-three-years-of-wartime-connectivity-taught-us)). Poland alone finances **more than 29,000 Starlink terminals** for Ukraine at roughly **US$50 million per year**, about half of Ukraine's network — a dependency Poland's foreign minister has repeatedly framed as a mere vendor relationship, threatening to "look for other suppliers" when SpaceX proves unreliable ([Tech-Insider/Sikorski analysis](https://tech-insider.org/sikorski-musk-starlink-politics-ukraine-2026/)).

Access is carefully managed but ultimately foreign-controlled. Ukraine's Ministry of Defence maintains **whitelists of approved terminals** submitted to SpaceX for verification; terminals bought by civilians lose connectivity above ~90 km/h; and the service is geofenced to operate only within Ukraine's internationally recognized borders, not over Russia — a restriction designed to deny Russian forces use of captured terminals, but also a demonstration of how a single company's policy choices shape Ukrainian operations ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813)). Germany's military space commander had warned days before the October incident that Russian satellites could threaten Starlink in orbit — and indeed, on 19 October, Russia's **Kosmos-2612 approached within roughly 26 meters of ICEYE-X36**, the radar satellite Ukraine uses ([Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/spacex-starlink-speed-ukraine-drones/), [United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813)).

### 3.2 Why "just use another commercial constellation" isn't a full answer

Alternatives exist but each carries a gap. Germany has financed Ukraine's use of **Eutelsat (OneWeb) satellite internet** for about a year as a partial alternative, but OneWeb is considerably more expensive to use and its current-generation capacity is limited; the EU's sovereign **IRIS²** constellation — 348 satellites led by SES, Eutelsat and Hispasat — will not see its first Ka-band military satellites launched until **2029**, with full commercial operations around **mid-2032** ([Wikipedia/IRIS²](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS%C2%B2), [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/30/eutelsat-iris2-10-billion-revenue-starlink-rival/), [Eutelsat](https://www.eutelsat.com/media-press/media-centre/news/eutelsat-play-central-role-delivering-next-phase-europes-sovereign)). In other words, every existing European option is either commercial capacity you rent (OneWeb) or sovereign infrastructure that arrives in the 2030s (IRIS²).

The ICEYE–Nokia proposition targets the gap in between: **sovereign ownership on a national timeline**. Rather than joining a shared EU constellation, a customer nation owns its satellites, ground stations and terminals outright — "no dependency on a foreign commercial operator's terms of service," as ICEYE CEO Rafal Modrzewski put it — with systems designed to deliver "targeted, high-throughput, low-latency connectivity over priority mission areas" rather than global consumer broadband ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers)). That is a different product from Starlink by design: smaller, national, mission-focused, and — critically — controlled by the user state.


## 4. What Each Partner Actually Brings

### 4.1 ICEYE: from "People's Satellite" to a sovereign-systems factory

ICEYE's credibility with Ukraine is not new — it is four years deep and battle-tested. In August 2022, the Serhiy Prytula Charity Foundation redirected roughly **US$15–17 million** crowdfunded for Bayraktar drones into a contract giving Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) full tasking rights over one ICEYE SAR satellite plus privileged access to the whole constellation ([Prytula Foundation](https://prytulafoundation.org/en/about/projects/archive/peoples-satellite), [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/05/27/crowdfunded-ukrainian-satellite-that-helped-find-russian-landing-ship-minsk-has-now-delivered-5900-radar-images-to-intelligence/)). That "People's Satellite" has since delivered **more than 5,900 radar images** to Ukrainian intelligence — through clouds, smoke and darkness at up to 25 cm resolution — credited with helping detect and destroy thousands of units of Russian equipment, including support for the strike on the landing ship Minsk in Sevastopol ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/05/27/crowdfunded-ukrainian-satellite-that-helped-find-russian-landing-ship-minsk-has-now-delivered-5900-radar-images-to-intelligence/), [Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/05/27/8036615/)). ICEYE also signed a Memorandum of Cooperation with Ukraine's MoD in July 2024 and committed that imagery of Ukraine's territory is never shared with hostile entities ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-strengthens-cooperation-with-the-ministry-of-defense-of-ukraine)).

The industrial proof point for the satcom venture is **Poland's MikroSAR program**: ICEYE signed a ~€200 million contract with Poland's Armament Agency in May 2025 and handed over a fully operational four-satellite sovereign SAR constellation — built, launched, qualified and transferred to Polish military operators — **in under 12 months**, which ICEYE calls the fastest deployment of an operational satellite program in the world ([PR Newswire/ICEYE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/iceye-delivers-mikrosar-system-to-polish-armed-forces-in-under-12-months-302773631.html), [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/air/iceye-hands-over-four-sar-satellites-to-poland-as-part-of-mikrosar-system)). The production engine behind that pace is scaling hard: ICEYE had launched **76 satellites** by July 2026, targets doubling production from 50 to **100 satellites per year by 2027**, and operates the world's largest SAR fleet — 82 X-band SAR satellites launched as of late 2026, including Gen4 spacecraft with 16 cm resolution ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-launches-four-new-satellites-aboard-transporter-17), [ESA Earth Online](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/missions/iceye)). Modrzewski's claim that "we delivered Poland its own radar satellite system in under a year — partnering with Nokia lets us move at a similar speed for satellite communications" is the venture's central promise ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers)).

### 4.2 Nokia: the networking, terminals and standards layer

Nokia's role is the part of the deal that connects directly to the patent and R&D analysis this research series examined earlier. The partnership "brings together ICEYE's track record delivering sovereign satellite missions with Nokia's extensive experience in secure networking technologies and growing defense portfolio," integrating **satellites, ground infrastructure and ruggedized end-user terminals** ([ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-and-nokia-partner-to-launch-secure-and-sovereign-satellite-communications-for-governments-and-defense-customers)). Concretely, Nokia's defense catalog already includes the **Banshee 5G Tactical Radio** — a portable, encrypted "network in a box" delivering up to 800 Mbps at the tactical edge with meshed MANET and SATCOM backhaul compatibility — and the **Mission-Safe Phone**, a MIL-STD-810H defense smartphone made in Europe; Nokia also runs a dedicated Space and Defense unit and created a Chairman of Nokia Defense role, now held by Chief Geopolitical Officer Mikko Hautala, who personally signed off the Ukraine announcement ([Nokia](https://www.nokia.com/newsroom/nokia-expands-defense-portfolio-with-two-advanced-tactical-communication-solutions/), [Nokia Banshee](https://www.nokia.com/defense/tactical-communications-solutions/banshee-tactical-radio/)).

Less visible but strategically deeper is Nokia's **non-terrestrial-network (NTN) standards position**. Nokia has publicly urged 3GPP to build satellite connectivity into 6G "from day one," provides base-station technology to AST SpaceMobile's direct-to-device program, and its standards research lead for NTN, Jeroen Wigard, personally holds **more than 150 patent applications** in the field; Nokia Bell Labs is actively publishing constellation-optimization research for LEO/MEO broadband ([Kratos/Space](https://www.kratosspace.com/constellations/articles/nokia-urges-3gpp-to-integrate-satellite-into-6g-from-day-1), [Next G Alliance](https://nextgalliance.org/technology-roadmap-working-group-ntn-panel/), [arXiv/FTA-NTN](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19078v1)). In the language of the earlier patent-portfolio analysis: satellite and defense connectivity is precisely the kind of adjacent domain where Nokia's deep cellular SEP estate and secure-networking IP can be levered into a new growth market — the ICEYE venture turns that portfolio logic into a product line, and Ukraine is its flagship potential customer.

## 5. The Honest Assessment: Strengths, Gaps, and What to Watch

### 5.1 Strengths and open gaps

The venture's strengths are unusual for a "letter of intent" story: a proven **12-month sovereign-delivery track record** (Poland), a production line scaling toward one satellite per week, a networking partner with end-to-end defense comms products, a financing-adjacent political framework (the €300M Orpo-visit package), and a customer with four years of operational ICEYE integration already in place ([PR Newswire/ICEYE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/iceye-delivers-mikrosar-system-to-polish-armed-forces-in-under-12-months-302773631.html), [Nordic Defence Sector](https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/iceye-and-nokia-sign-loi-with-ukraine-on-sovereign-satellite-communications)). The gaps are equally concrete: **no disclosed funding, constellation size, spectrum plan, or Ukraine-specific schedule**; a 2028 first-launch horizon for the joint system; and the fact that satellite *communications* — high-throughput LEO broadband payloads, terminals, gateway ground stations — is a new business for ICEYE, whose heritage is radar imaging, not comms ([Telecoms Tech News](https://www.telecomstechnews.com/news/iceye-and-nokia-sign-ukraine-sovereign-satellite-letter-of-intent/), [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/air/ukraine-explores-sovereign-satellite-network-with-iceye-and-nokia)).

There is also a layered-dependency irony worth watching: ICEYE's satellites, including those for sovereign customers, currently ride to orbit on **SpaceX Falcon 9 rideshare missions**, and its terminals' silicon includes US-origin components — sovereignty at the operations layer does not yet mean sovereignty at the launch and supply-chain layer ([ESA Earth Online](https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/missions/iceye), [ICEYE](https://www.iceye.com/newsroom/press-releases/iceye-launches-four-new-satellites-aboard-transporter-17)). And on the threat side, the October near-approach of Russia's Kosmos-2612 to within ~26 meters of ICEYE-X36 is a reminder that any Ukrainian-owned constellation becomes an orbital target from day one, requiring resilience design — many cheap satellites, rapid replenishment, cyber-hardened ground segments — rather than a few exquisite birds ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraines-drones-were-hit-by-a-10-day-starlink-speed-limit-what-happened-and-why-it-matters-23813)).

### 5.2 What to watch next

The signposts that will tell us whether this becomes real are: **(1)** conversion of the LoI into a funded procurement contract with a stated satellite count and delivery schedule — the single most important milestone; **(2)** disclosure of the ICEYE–Nokia system's technical architecture (frequency bands, terminal form factors, throughput per satellite) when the 2028 first-launch campaign firms up; **(3)** whether other frontline states (Poland, the Baltics, Sweden — already an ICEYE defense customer via its January 2026 FMV agreement) sign similar satcom LoIs, creating a federated Northern European sovereign-comms bloc ([Nordic Defence Sector](https://nordicdefencesector.com/en/article/iceye-and-nokia-sign-loi-with-ukraine-on-sovereign-satellite-communications)); and **(4)** how Kyiv sequences this against its existing OneWeb arrangement and eventual IRIS² access, since the realistic end-state is a layered architecture — Starlink for mass coverage, OneWeb/IRIS² for European backbone, and a national ICEYE–Nokia layer for the most sensitive, contested-mission traffic where Ukrainian control is non-negotiable.

The bottom line for Ukraine: the October crisis proved that connectivity rented from a foreign private operator can be degraded by decisions Kyiv cannot see, predict, or appeal — for ten days, with tens of millions of dollars in operational cost and lives put at risk. The ICEYE–Nokia LoI is the first concrete step toward a layer of the battlefield network that answers to Kyiv alone. It is years from orbit and nothing is yet funded — but for the first time, the path from dependence to sovereignty has names, dates, and a factory behind it.
